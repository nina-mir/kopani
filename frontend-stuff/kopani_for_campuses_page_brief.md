# Kopani “For Campuses” Page Brief

**Purpose:** Build a focused `/campuses` page that makes Kopani look like a real business, not just a beautiful literary browsing app.

**Core thesis:** Kopani is the AI-native discovery, reputation, and compensation infrastructure for independent literature. The campus reading center is the credible wedge: universities, MFA programs, libraries, writing centers, and English departments already need contemporary reading material, already have budgets, and already understand curated databases.

---

## 1. What the page needs to accomplish

The page should answer five questions quickly:

1. **What is Kopani for campuses?**  
   A searchable, rights-respecting reading center for contemporary independent literature.

2. **Who is it for?**  
   English departments, creative writing programs, MFA programs, libraries, writing centers, literary centers, humanities institutes, student journals, and faculty building contemporary syllabi.

3. **What problem does it solve?**  
   Contemporary literary work is scattered across journals, hard to search by theme/contributor/genre/reading time, and difficult to turn into class-ready reading lists.

4. **Why is Kopani different?**  
   Kopani does not republish copyrighted body text. It builds metadata, discovery, reading lists, contributor maps, and compensation tools around the original publications.

5. **What is the next action?**  
   Request a campus pilot, join the pilot waitlist, or contact Kopani about a department/library demo.

### Section 2 — The problem

**Heading:**  
`The best contemporary writing is hard to assign.`

**Copy:**  
`Independent literary journals publish extraordinary fiction, essays, poetry, interviews, translations, and visual work. But the work is scattered across hundreds of sites, often hidden after publication, and rarely searchable in ways that match how teachers actually build courses: by theme, contributor, form, length, journal, or classroom fit.`

**Three problem cards:**

1. **Discovery is fragmented**  
   `Faculty and students search journal by journal, tab by tab, with no shared map of the field.`

2. **Classroom fit is invisible**  
   `Reading time, genre, contributor roles, translation, visual art, and keywords are rarely available in one place.`

3. **Writers are disconnected from value**  
   `When a piece travels into classrooms or reading groups, that cultural value rarely creates direct support for writers, translators, artists, or journals.`

### Section 3 — The product

**Heading:**  
`A searchable reading center for the independent literary press.`

**Copy:**  
`Kopani turns scattered literary publishing into structured, searchable cultural infrastructure. Students and faculty can search across titles, journals, contributors, translators, visual artists, content types, keywords, and reading-time metadata — then follow each piece back to its original publication.`

**Feature cards:**

1. **Metadata-rich discovery**

2. **Classroom-friendly browsing**

3. **Rights-respecting links**  
   `Kopani links readers to original journals instead of republishing copyrighted body text.`

4. **Contributor-aware index**  
   `Writers, translators, and visual artists are treated as first-class contributors, not hidden metadata.`

### Section 5 — The business model

**Heading:**  
`A clear campus pilot model.`

**Copy:**  
`Kopani starts with annual campus reading center subscriptions for departments, libraries, and writing programs. The early pilot focuses on discovery, reading-list workflows, classroom use, and feedback from faculty and students.`

**Suggested pilot pricing language:**

`Campus pilots: $5,000–$15,000/year depending on program size, number of seats, and support needs.`

**Do not overpromise.** Keep the language “pilot” and “early access.” This sounds credible and avoids pretending the enterprise product is fully built.

**What a campus pilot includes:**

- Access to Kopani’s searchable independent literature index
- Genre and contributor browsing
- Reading-time sorting for classroom planning
- Curated starter reading lists
- Faculty feedback sessions
- Optional campus-branded collection page
- Future access to saved lists, cohorts, and assignment analytics

---

### Section 6 — The bigger vision

**Heading:**  
`From discovery to direct writer support.`

**Copy:**  
`Kopani begins as a campus discovery layer, but the larger opportunity is the reputation and compensation layer for independent literature. As readers, faculty, students, and journals save, assign, discuss, and support pieces, Kopani can build the cultural graph that shows how independent writing travels — and eventually route money back to writers, translators, visual artists, and journals.`

**Vision bullets:**

- Campus reading lists become signals of literary reputation.
- Faculty picks and student saves become discovery data.
- Patron dedications and literary micro-grants create a direct compensation path.
- Journals receive new readership instead of being replaced.
- Writers, translators, visual artists, and publishers remain at the center.

**Important phrasing:**  
Use `patron dedications` or `literary micro-grants`, not `auction`, on the public page. “Auction” can sound legally and operationally complex. “Dedication” sounds literary, humane, and mission-aligned.

---

### Section 7 — Rights-respecting principle

**Heading:**  
`Built around the original publication.`

**Copy:**  
`Kopani does not replace independent journals. It sends readers back to them. The platform indexes metadata, contributors, keywords, reading time, and discovery signals while preserving the original publication as the canonical reading destination.`

**Small principle statement:**  
`Discovery should create more value for writers and journals, not extract attention away from them.`

This section is important because the competition is AI-focused. Judges may be sensitive to copyright and publishing concerns. Make your rights posture explicit.

## 7. The judge-impressing angle

The judges will not be impressed merely because the site looks nice. They will be impressed if the app shows:

1. **A real wedge**  
   Campuses are a concrete first customer.

2. **A compounding data asset**  
   Search, saves, reading lists, faculty picks, assignments, and patron support become the literary reputation graph.

3. **A transaction layer**  
   Patron dedications and micro-grants create the path to direct writer compensation.

4. **A rights-aware approach**  
   Kopani sends readers to original journals instead of replacing them.

5. **AI-native operations**  
   Perplexity Computer is used for market research, customer discovery, competitive analysis, outreach, pitch writing, and product iteration.

---

## 8. What not to build before the deadline

Do not build:

- Real Stripe payments
- Real auctions
- Real institutional accounts
- Full auth if it risks breaking the app
- Complex analytics
- Complex CMS/admin tools
- More ingestion work unless the current demo data is broken

The goal is not feature count. The goal is a credible company demo.
